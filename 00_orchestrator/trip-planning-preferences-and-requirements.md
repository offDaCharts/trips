# Trip Planning Preferences and Requirements

Use this note as the master brief for any future trip-specific research thread, subagent, HTML page, slide deck, or comparison pass.

## Core Trip Shape

- Travelers: two adults plus one baby who will be about one year old.
- Flight pricing: price two paid adult tickets, with the baby as infant-in-lap unless explicitly comparing the cost of a third seat.
- Origin airport: Atlanta, with Delta preferred first. Delta partners are acceptable when sold through Delta or when they make the itinerary materially better.
- Date target: August 31 to September 13, 2026. A one or two day shift is acceptable only if it clearly improves flights, lodging, or route quality.
- Trip length: about 12 to 14 days.
- Lodging rhythm: aim for 3 to 5 bases total, avoiding one-night hops unless they solve an airport buffer, ferry/flight timing, or a special splurge.
- Budget target: around $10,000. Slightly over is acceptable, but avoid plans that are way over budget unless clearly labeled as a premium comparison option.

## Travel Style

The ideal trip should feel outdoorsy, scenic, active, comfortable, and baby-aware.

Preferred ingredients:

- mountains, lakes, alpine villages, coastlines, glaciers, waterfalls, farms, and animals
- easy-to-moderate hikes, viewpoints, scenic drives, gondolas, lake walks, and short nature excursions
- farm stays, farm-to-table food, family-run inns, countryside resorts, and places where the baby can crawl, explore, and see animals
- wellness or spa moments mixed into the trip, especially after travel days or several active days
- a few memorable high-comfort stays, balanced by practical/value bases

The Switzerland reference vibe matters: Zermatt, Matterhorn views, Interlaken, Zurich, and the romantic/personal feeling of Nancy are useful inspiration points, but new trips should not simply repeat the same locations.

## Baby Logistics

Every day should be evaluated through the lens of traveling with a one-year-old.

For each day, note:

- stroller-friendly, carrier-friendly, or carrier-required
- where the baby can safely crawl, play, or reset
- whether the day has too much car time
- whether naps can happen in the car, stroller, hotel, or carrier
- crib availability or need to request baby gear
- grocery/laundry convenience when relevant
- backup plan for bad weather, low energy, or baby schedule disruption

Avoid designing a trip where the baby is only passively carried from hike to hike. The plan should include days with farms, lakeshores, parks, hotel grounds, pools, spas, short walks, playgrounds, animal encounters, or relaxed village time.

## Booking-Grade Research Standard

The goal is not a loose inspiration board. The goal is: if this trip wins, the itinerary should be close to bookable.

Each trip should include:

- exact recommended flights, including airline, flight number when available, departure/arrival airports, times, layovers, cabin/fare note, and two-adult cash price snapshot
- two or three flight alternatives only when they are genuinely useful
- exact lodging choice for each base, with nightly rate, room type if visible, taxes/fees if visible, cancellation/refund note, and direct booking link
- exact activity plan for each day, with cost, hours/season notes, travel time, baby suitability, and source link
- exact or realistic food budget per day, ideally with named restaurant/grocery options
- transport plan, including rental car pickup/dropoff dates, train/ferry/flight legs, parking notes, and whether transport cost is a one-day charge or allocated across multiple days
- daily cost stack: lodging, transport, activities, food, and day total
- total trip cost rollup
- explicit notes for optional nicer/more expensive alternatives

Use estimates only where live booking data is unavailable, and label them clearly as estimates.

## Itinerary Design Rules

Each trip route should make geographic sense.

Planning priorities:

- reduce backtracking
- avoid too many long travel days in a row
- place slower/reset days after flights, long drives, or big excursions
- use airport buffer nights when needed
- cluster activities around each base
- choose bases because they unlock specific experiences, not just because they are famous
- explain why the trip goes in this order

Each day needs enough substance to deserve its own page.

For each day, include:

- a clear title that says what the day is actually about
- a short pitch explaining why this day is worth doing
- primary activity
- backup/weather option
- lodging for the night
- transport leg and travel time
- food plan
- baby notes
- cost line items
- booking priority or decision note
- source links
- at least one relevant image

Avoid vague lines like "explore area" or "mountain day." Say what place, why it matters, what the family would actually do there, and what makes it special.

## Visual and App Requirements

Every trip should work well in the local HTML trip app.

Required pages:

- homepage comparison card
- trip overview page
- one page per day

Trip overview should show:

- route summary
- total cost
- flight summary
- lodging summary
- route rationale
- map with real OpenStreetMap tiles
- route lines following actual roads where practical
- clickable numbered day pins
- day list with a unique thumbnail image per day

Each day page should show:

- hero image for that specific day
- day summary and pitch
- schedule
- lodging details
- activity details
- food plan
- transport plan
- baby logistics
- cost stack
- source links
- map highlighting where that day falls on the route
- previous day, next day, and back-to-overview navigation

Image rules:

- every day needs a unique image or image set related to that specific day
- avoid repeating generic images across multiple days
- cache images locally in the app assets folder
- record image source/attribution in the data
- replace broken or low-relevance images immediately

Map rules:

- maps must be real maps, not abstract line diagrams
- numbered pins should be readable and not overlap badly
- clicking a pin should open a tooltip with day number, date, short pitch/snippet, thumbnail, and link to the full day
- route lines should follow realistic driving/train/ferry/flight paths where practical; use straight/dashed reference lines only for unavailable or non-road segments

## Research Sources

Use a mix of inspiration sources and authoritative validation.

Good inspiration sources:

- Ryan Shirley destination videos and transcripts when available
- reputable travel guide videos and blogs for idea generation
- tourism-board suggested itineraries

Validation sources:

- airline sites, especially Delta when possible
- hotel direct sites
- official tourism boards
- national park or official attraction sites
- ferry, train, rental car, lift/gondola, and activity operators
- restaurant, grocery, farm, spa, and family attraction sites

If an idea comes from a video, link to the video and timestamp where possible, but validate final decisions with official or direct sources before putting them in the plan.

## Comparison Criteria

When comparing trip options, score or comment on:

- overall vibe match
- baby friendliness
- scenery and outdoor payoff
- farm/animal/farm-to-table potential
- wellness/spa potential
- lodging quality and comfort
- route simplicity
- travel-day difficulty
- weather risk for the dates
- budget fit
- novelty compared with prior Switzerland trip
- how ready the plan is to book
